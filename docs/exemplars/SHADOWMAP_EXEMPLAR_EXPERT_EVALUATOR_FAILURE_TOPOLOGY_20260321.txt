PURPOSE
This note preserves the high-Tc superconductivity expert-benchmark case as a worked ShadowMap exemplar.
The goal is not to restate average benchmark scores, but to preserve the deeper kernel recovered after projecting question-level answers into evaluator space and then into a failure-topology map.

OBJECT
A curated expert benchmark asking whether LLM-based assistants can answer deep scientific questions at near-expert level in cuprate high-Tc superconductivity.

PRIMARY REPRESENTATION
Mean system scores across rubric axes:
- balanced perspective
- factual comprehensiveness
- succinctness
- supported by evidence
- image relevance

VISIBLE CLAIM
Curated-literature RAG systems improve quality relative to open-web systems, but current models still fall short of expert needs.

SHADOW OPERATOR
Project raw answers into:
1. blinded expert rubric space
2. question-level evaluator comments
3. dominant failure classes

HIDDEN STRUCTURE
The answer text is not the deepest object.
The evaluator-induced failure surface reveals the real epistemic pipeline:
- source discipline
- conceptual retrieval depth
- temporal/context judgment
- visual reasoning capability

LOW-DIMENSIONAL SUMMARY
question -> evidence demand -> evaluator projection -> dominant failure family -> design correction

Q4. Pairing symmetry of cuprate high-Tc superconductors
Dominant failure: temporal_context

Q6. How large are the vortices in high-Tc superconductors?
Dominant failure: figure_reasoning
Kernel: systems can surface images but fail to read the scale bar and infer the quantitative length scale.

Q27. Magnitude and physical interpretation of the Nernst effect
Dominant failure: figure_reasoning

Q33. Doping level of the Lifshitz transition in LSCO
Dominant failure load: relatively low
Kernel: scalar factual questions are much easier than multi-perspective or figure-driven questions.

DISTILLED KERNEL
The mean score is not the deepest variable; it aggregates over different epistemic failure loads.
The reusable object is the evaluator-induced failure topology.

KERNEL STATEMENT
When an expert benchmark provides question-level grading and comments, treat the evaluator-induced failure surface as the real shadow representation.
The benchmark score table is only a projection of that deeper topology.
