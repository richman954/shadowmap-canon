PURPOSE
This file canonizes a reusable local ShadowMap procedure for planar domains bounded by finitely many lines and/or circles where the PDE kernel is opaque in the native cell picture but becomes explicit after reflection-orbit construction.

KERNEL STATEMENT
The geometric cell is not the deepest object; it generates a reflection orbit.
The orbit carries the PDE kernel.

TRIGGER CONDITIONS
Use this submodule when all or most of the following hold:
1. The object is a planar domain or cell bounded by finitely many generalized circles (lines and circles).
2. The native geometric picture shows the cell but not the operational kernel.
3. Reflection across the boundaries generates a finite parqueting or a finitely compressible orbit.
4. The target quantity is a Schwarz, Green, Neumann, or related boundary-value kernel.

PRIMARY REPRESENTATION
A single planar cell / tile / wedge / lens / circular polygon.

SHADOW OPERATOR
Repeated reflection at boundary lines and circles.
- One reflection acts inversively.
- Two reflections compose to a Mobius transformation.

SHADOW SPACE
Not the original cell alone, but:
- the orbit of reflected cells, and
- the orbit of a point under the reflection generators.

CORE SPLIT
The trace of a point generally separates into two classes:
- Mobius-image class
- Inversive-image class
These classes contribute differently to the compressed kernel product.

KERNEL ASSEMBLY RULE
For finite parqueting / finite reflected-cell cases:
1. Generate the reflected trace of z.
2. Compress that orbit into a product P(z,zeta) for Green-type kernels or Q(z,zeta) for Neumann-type kernels.
3. Evaluate:
   G(z,zeta) = log |P(z,zeta)|^2
   N(z,zeta) = -log |Q(z,zeta)|^2

COMPRESSION RULE
The visible geometry is compressed to:
cell -> reflection orbit -> orbit product -> kernel

STANDARD SHADOWMAP FORM (LOCAL VERSION)
1. Object: finite reflected circular-polygon cell
2. Primary representation: one visible cell
3. Opacity: kernel not legible in the native cell picture
4. Shadow operator: repeated reflections at lines/circles
5. Shadow space: orbit of reflected cells + point trace orbit
6. Hidden structure recovered: image algebra organizing poles, zeros, boundary cancellation
7. Invariant recovered: compressed orbit product P or Q
8. Governing math: inversive geometry, Mobius transformations, meromorphic products, conformal/inversive invariance
9. Practical use: explicit construction of Green / Neumann / related kernels
10. Verdict: strong when orbit closes finitely or compresses cleanly
11. Next best move: instantiate on a new geometry and test closure/compression

REUSABLE PROCEDURE
INPUT:
- Domain D bounded by finitely many lines/circles
- Interior points z, zeta
- Boundary-value kernel target (Green or Neumann)

ALGORITHM:
1. Encode each boundary piece as a generalized circle.
2. Build the reflection generators.
3. Generate the reflected orbit of the cell.
4. Generate the reflected trace orbit of z.
5. Separate direct/Mobius-type versus inversive-type images when needed.
6. Assemble the compressed orbit product P or Q.
7. Evaluate G or N from the log-magnitude rule.
8. Transport the result across equivalent reflected cells by symmetry.
9. If the orbit is infinite, test convergence before canonizing the result.

OUTPUTS
- Explicit kernel formula when finite closure occurs
- Reflection-orbit representation when closed form is not yet compressed
- Low-dimensional kernel statement for future reuse

LIMITS
- Strongest in finite parqueting / finitely compressible cases
- Infinite parqueting requires convergence analysis
- Do not over-assign exact factor-to-image provenance unless the source explicitly tracks it
